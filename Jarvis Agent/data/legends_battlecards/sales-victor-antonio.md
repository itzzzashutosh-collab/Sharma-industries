## 2. BUFFETT × SWATCH SYNTHESIS: THE COMPOUNDING FLOAT MODEL

Warren's principle: *"The key to investing is pricing the future return relative to the present cost of capital."* Applied to Swatch's dealer network:

**Each dealer is a micro-float unit.** Their working capital is borrowed at near-zero cost (7-day credit). Swatch earns returns on dealer capital, not its own.

| Component | Swatch Advantage |
|---|---|
| Float Source | Dealer's ₹690 × volume → earns Swatch spread |
| Float Cost | 7-day credit = **₹0 interest** |
| Economic Moat | 65% margin deters MNC competition; dealers become loyal |
| Compounding Layer | Repeat orders every 25–30 days → annual cycle = 12–15 turns |

**The Flywheel:** Higher margin → faster turns → more cash flow back to dealer → they reinvest in Swatch stock → Swatch gets volume → economies of scale → stronger margins.
